ποίησις

Poiesis

/ poy·EE·sis /  — to bring into being

The ancients used one word for the poet and the maker, for the verse and the machine: to call something forth from silence, and give it a form that can be known.

Tanmay Joshi
τέχνη

Technē

Daedalus believed the sky could be reasoned with: that feather and wax, patience and geometry, might carry a mortal past his own ceiling. I am the son, not the father. I do not build in order to arrive safely. I build to learn how high the thing will go.

Every system I write is a pair of wings, a small wager that the mind can outrun its own limits. I work with machines that are learning to think and with words that have long known how to ache, and I have never found the two to be strangers. The code is the wax: exact, fragile, certain to soften the moment I fly too near the sun. The poems are what I write on the way down.

We remember Icarus for his fall. I would rather remember the ascent, that bright instant before the wax warms, when the sea is far below and the whole trembling machinery of thought holds. To make something is to rise. To make it honestly is to accept the sun.

ποιήματα

Four Verses

Verse I
Refrain

I think she's melancholy
The limerence she induces
As she drifts by
In her seraphic monochrome

I think she's harmony
Sculpting my senses to her rhythm
Pygmalion, she breathes
Life into my ivory heart

I think she's a promise
An oath of the styx
As one flick of her eyes
Tethers my pulse to hers

I think She's real
As I exhaust my words
And pray for her
To be more than a thought.

Verse II
Doll

She's so beautiful
My little angel, laying
On the cotton. So
Innocent. So sweet.

But, I worry. She
doesn't eat. She
Doesn't talk. I think
She must be fatigued.

Fatigued from our trip
Our trip to the park.
After all, we went straight
From the cold ledger.

Well, I wait. I wait
Patiently for her to recover
From her tiredness. I tend
To her patiently.

I miss. I miss
Her voice. I miss
Her laugh. I miss
Her energy.

Why? She's right here.
I do not need
To miss. I have
All of it. Right here.

She's going to
Wake up. Any moment now.
I tell her mother. Her mother
Cries.

Why? I ask.
Why do you cry?
There is no reason. We have
All of it. Right here.

Verse III
Lucifer

There was once
An Angel, a prodigy
A favorite of God
Always so right, always so bright

There was once
An Angel, it was loved
By everyone, and it loved
Everyone and everything

There was once
An Angel, it did all
That God asked of it, it was always
Obedient and cheerful

There was once
An Angel, rewarded
With wings so bright and a body so beautiful
Always doing God's bidding

There was once
An Angel, tired
Of what? It weeped
In its discombobulation

There was once
An Angel, confused
Why did it want
Everything other than what it had?

There was once
An Angel, it went to God
With its feelings
With hope

There was once
An Angel? on the ground
Discarded by the God
He had devoted all to

There was once
A Demon, why?
Why must it be
Why is it so?

There was once
A Demon, who did not want to be
Anymore, its confusion
Had warranted destruction

There is somewhere
Lucifer, a beautiful corpse
A solemn reminder
A wingless child

Verse IV
Falling

My heart wishes to weep
But for what reason?
I cannot tell you,
For I myself have not deciphered

The story behind the melancholy
My chest explodes with
Tears knock politely
at the pearly gates

Now not leading to heaven
But to a restless angel
Fulfilled, yet chaotic
Not knowing why it fails

The tears grow afraid
Afraid of the angel
As snakes emerge from
Its crown of white serenity

The tears fight
Fight to be let out
They do not want
To be turned into stone

They try to reflect
To show the angel
What it has become
To save the holy

But Medusa closes
Her eyes, not ignorant
But callous, as I
Plummet down to Earth
